# Bio Writing Specification

This spec applies to all bios in datasets built on `test-data-factory`. Individual
data packages may add a short addendum for terminology or sensitivity guidance
specific to their subject matter — see each package's own `BIO_WRITING_SPEC.md`.

## What a Bio SHOULD Be:

### 1. Respectful and Substantive

- Honor real people whose lives and work the dataset represents
- Acknowledge the systemic context they faced (oppression, discrimination,
  barriers, marginalization — as appropriate to the dataset)
- 3–4 sentences providing meaningful context about their work and impact
- Recognize their specific contributions in their own field

### 2. Factual and Grounded

- Focus on actual work, achievements, and ongoing efforts
- Describe the specific systems or barriers they confronted
- Acknowledge the historical context of their work
- Use concrete examples (activism, research, inventions, organizing, etc.)

### 3. Varied Opening Approaches

- Use diverse sentence structures and openings
- Avoid repetitive formulas across different entries
- Each bio should feel unique to that individual

### 4. Realistic About Impact

- Describe concrete changes or contributions where applicable
- Acknowledge when problems persist or struggles continue
- Avoid suggesting individual actions "solved" systemic issues
- Recognize the collaborative or ongoing nature of their work

## What a Bio Should NOT Be:

### 1. Formulaic

- Avoid repetitive patterns like "The X who Y'ed"
- Don't use the same sentence structure across multiple entries
- Reject tagline or marketing-style language
- No cookie-cutter approaches

### 2. Pithy or Trivializing

- No inspirational poster quotes or oversimplified conclusions
- Avoid suggesting systemic problems have been "solved"
- Don't reduce complex work to feel-good soundbites
- Reject superficial treatment of serious issues

### 3. Movie Taglines

- Not entertainment marketing copy
- Avoid dramatic one-liners that treat serious work lightly
- Don't prioritize "catching attention" over honoring the person
- These are real people, not fictional characters

### 4. Overly Triumphant

- Don't imply individual actions dismantled entire systems
- Acknowledge ongoing nature of systemic problems
- Avoid language that suggests struggles are over
- Recognize that the issues they confronted often continue today

## Tone Guidelines:

- **Dignified and respectful** — These are real people who deserve honor
- **Acknowledges complexity** — Their work often involved difficult choices and
  ongoing challenges
- **Honors courage and creativity** — Recognize the personal risks they took
  and the originality of their contributions
- **Recognizes difficulty** — Don't minimize the systems or barriers they
  confronted
- **Present-aware** — Many of the issues they faced continue today
